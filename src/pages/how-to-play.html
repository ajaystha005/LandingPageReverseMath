How to play ReverseMath

ReverseMath is a reverse math puzzle: you start from the target and work out how to reach it. In a normal sum you get the numbers and work out the answer. Here you get the answer, called the target, and you work out where the numbers go.

Every puzzle shows a target, an equation with empty blanks, and a few number tiles. Drag or tap each tile into a blank. Use every number once. When the equation comes out to exactly the target, you win the puzzle.

A first puzzle

Target: 14. Equation: blank × blank + blank. Numbers: 3, 2 and 4.

Try 3 × 4 + 2. Multiplying comes first, so 3 × 4 is 12. Then add 2. That makes 14. You hit the target.

Try 3 × 2 + 4 instead and you get 10, not 14. Swap two numbers and try again.

Real math rules: the order matters

ReverseMath uses the normal order of operations. Multiplying and dividing are worked out before adding and subtracting.

Here is a puzzle that shows why. Target: 17. Equation: blank + blank × blank. Numbers: 2, 3 and 5.

Put in 2 + 3 × 5. The 3 × 5 happens first and gives 15. Then 2 + 15 is 17.

If you worked from left to right, you would do 2 + 3 first, then × 5, and get 25. That is a different answer.

What is a reverse math puzzle?

A reverse math puzzle is one where you start from the answer. You see a target and an equation with blanks, and you place the numbers so the equation comes out to the target.

Tips for solving faster

  • Work backwards. Ask: what do I need to add or take away to land on the target?
  • Made a wrong move? Undo is there in every mode.
